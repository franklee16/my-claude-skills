# Referee-Response Letter Style Guide — Weikai Li

## How to Use This Guide

This guide distills the referee-response letter style of **Weikai Li** (City University of Hong Kong) from six of his actual R&R letters (empirical asset pricing, behavioral finance, ESG). An AI assistant drafting a future response letter in his voice should consult this guide *before* writing: start with the **Letter Skeleton (§2)** for overall structure, match the referee comment to a bucket in the **Comment-Handling Taxonomy (§4)**, then pull phrasings from the **Concession (§5)** and **Pushback (§6)** phrase banks. Every verbatim quote carries a source tag `[P1]`–`[P6]`; copy these templates and fill in the substance — never invent new stylistic phrasings. Convergent patterns (4–6 of 6 letters) are **core** defaults; one-off habits are flagged as **occasional variants**.

**Source pairs cited as [P1]–[P6]:**
- [P1] *Security Analysts and Capital Market Anomalies* (JFE #2018-0991)
- [P2] *Is Carbon Risk Priced in the Cross-Section of Corporate Bond Returns?* (JFQA #22-22716, referee 1)
- [P3] *Is Carbon Risk Priced…* (JFQA #22-22716, referee "z") — **same paper/authors as [P2]**; their shared templates (dual-pointer references, "Online Appendix" capitalization, closing ritual) reflect one authorial voice, so they should be weighted as ~1.5 independent sources rather than 2 when judging consensus.
- [P4] *Short Selling ETFs* (#20210252, RAPS)
- [P5] *Gender Effects of COVID: Evidence from Equity Analysts* (RAST)
- [P6] *Inside Broker* (JFE)

**The six convergent patterns (the spine of the style — anchor everything else on these):**
1. Referee comment reproduced verbatim, in full, in *italics*, before the response — universal across all 6.
2. `"Thank you for X"` + `"Following your suggestion, we have [past participle]"` as the default adoption opener — universal.
3. Pushback via reframing, never direct negation — universal. No letter contains "we respectfully disagree" or "you are wrong."
4. Hedged verbs carry the disagreement; results/tables do the arguing (`"seems to suggest"`, `"at a minimum"`, `"not necessarily inconsistent"`).
5. Precise manuscript pointers (Section / page / footnote / Table / Equation) for every change — universal.
6. R-prefixed response-only exhibits (Tables R1…Rn, Figure R.1) duplicated into the letter so the referee need not open the manuscript — present in [P1], [P3], [P4], [P6].

## 1. Voice Summary (One Paragraph)

Weikai Li's response-letter persona is **warmly deferential, evidentiary, and never adversarial**. Every comment — major or minor — is opened with a "Thank you for…" formula before any substance begins, and the referee is consistently positioned as an intellectual ally whose suggestions improved the paper ([P1], [P2], [P3], [P4], [P5], [P6]). Disagreement is **never delivered as direct negation**; instead the author concedes the question's importance, then lets new tables, robustness checks, or reframings do the disagreeing — pivoting on "However," "While," and "Although" rather than "we respectfully disagree" ([P1], [P2], [P3], [P4], [P5], [P6]). The voice is first-person plural ("we have now…," "we obtain…"), formal-professional with a thin layer of genuine warmth ("insightful," "constructive," "excellent suggestion"), and laced with explicit epistemic humility ("we conjecture," "at a minimum," "may not be able to fully," "we do not have causal evidence") that doubles as a defense of the contribution ([P1], [P2], [P4]). Actions are stated declaratively in the present-perfect ("we have now moved / included / revised / corrected") and pinned to precise manuscript locations — section, page, footnote, table, even quoting the revised prose inline as proof of completion ([P2], [P3], [P4], [P6]). The signature structural choices are a **front-loaded summary of major changes** before point-by-point work (a 3/6 majority pattern — strongly recommended for heavy revisions), a consistent quoting convention (referee comment verbatim in *italics*, then a labeled response), and — when new analysis is produced for the letter rather than the manuscript — a dedicated **R-table / R-figure sequence** (Table R1, R2, …; Figure R.1) that mirrors and complements the manuscript tables ([P1], [P3], [P4], [P6]).

## 2. Letter Skeleton (Structural Template)

The canonical anatomy, distilled across all six letters. Steps marked **CORE** appear in nearly every letter; **OPTIONAL** / **DEFAULT** steps are flagged with the frequency and the recommended default.

```
[1. TITLE BLOCK]  (CORE)
   - Manuscript title (quoted), journal manuscript number, "(Revised)" tag.
   - Examples:
     * "Responses to the Report from the Referee on the Manuscript Titled
        'Security Analysts and Capital Market Anomalies' (JFE#2018-0991, Revised)"  [P1]
     * '"Is Carbon Risk Priced in the Cross-Section of Corporate Bond Returns?"
        (MS-JFQA-22-22716)'  [P2], [P3]
     * "Responses to Referee / 'Short Selling ETFs' (#20210252)"  [P4]
   - Include a bold **Date:** line when the journal convention expects it.  [P1]

[2. SALUTATION]  (OPTIONAL — pair-dependent; 1/6 explicit)
   - "Dear Referee," appears in [P6]; others open with no salutation and go
     straight into the framing paragraph [P2], [P3], [P5].
   - DEFAULT: include "Dear Referee," when writing to a single named referee;
     omit for editor-addressed or multi-referee packages.

[3. OPENING FRAMING PARAGRAPH]  (CORE, ~3–6 sentences)
   Three beats, almost always in this order:
     (a) Gratitude — "Thank you very much for… / We are grateful for…"
     (b) Claim of improvement — "the paper has clearly improved" / "greatly
         improved our paper" / "considerably improve the quality"
     (c) Formatting convention announced — "we reproduce your comments point
         by point in italics and then give our responses" / "We itemize your
         comments below in italics followed by our responses."
   Optional additional beats:
     - Apology-for-delay framed as effort-signaler [P6]: "We first apologize
       for the delay… this reflects how seriously we have taken your report."
     - Summary of revision activities (additional analyses, clarifications,
       expositional discussions) [P2], [P3].
   Note: address the referee in the SECOND PERSON ("you," "your") — the
   universal pattern in 5/6 letters. [P5]'s third-person "the referee" is a
   one-off variant; do not propagate it.

[4. SUMMARY OF MAJOR CHANGES]  (DEFAULT for heavy-revision letters;
   present in 3/6: [P1], [P4], [P6]; absent in [P2], [P3], [P5])
   - A front-loaded numbered digest BEFORE the point-by-point section, so the
     editor/referee can absorb the revision in 30 seconds. Frame items as
     concessions/adoptions, not defenses.
   - Numbering style varies: Arabic "1., 2., 3." [P1]; roman "(I), (II)…" [P4];
     or "1) 2) 3)" [P6]. Pick one and stay consistent within the letter.
   - Each item: (a) what the referee asked + (b) what was done + (c) manuscript
     pointer.
   - Label examples:
     * "Before responding to the specific points in your report, we would like
        to summarize our major changes in the paper:"  [P1]
     * "Summary of major changes in this version of the paper:"  [P1]
     * "Below, we give a summary of the main changes that are made before
        addressing each of your comments."  [P4]
     * "The main changes from the previous draft are:"  [P6]
   - When the revision is light, this block may be omitted (the [P2]/[P3]/[P5]
     austerity variant).

[5. BRIDGE / DETAILED-RESPONSES HEADER]  (CORE)
   - A second short preamble that re-thanks the referee and re-announces the
     italics-and-Response convention.
   - Examples:
     * "Detailed Responses to the Reviewer's Comments"  [P1]
     * "In response to comments in your report:"  [P2], [P3]
     * "Point-to-point responses"  [P4]
     * "For ease of reference, we paste your comments below in italics,
        followed by our response to each comment."  [P6]

[6. PER-COMMENT BLOCKS]  (CORE — repeat for each referee point)
   Order: follow the referee's OWN ordering and numbering exactly. Do NOT
   reorder or regroup.
   Two-bucket partition (when the referee separates Major / Minor):
     - "In response to comments in your report:" then later
       "In response to minor comments in your report:"  [P2]
   Each block:
     (a) Referee comment reproduced VERBATIM in *italics* (preserve original
         numbering, sub-bullets, comma style, nested structure).
     (b) Response label — three conventions (pick one per letter):
         * Bold **Response:** label on its own line [P1];
         * Plain prose reply (no label) [P2], [P3], [P5], [P6];
         * Bold topic label "Minor Concerns/Suggestions – [Topic]:" [P4].
     (c) Reply internal structure (the "four-beat"):
         1. Thanks / agreement ritual ("Thank you for… We agree that…")
         2. Statement of why the point matters
         3. Action taken or new evidence introduced
         4. Manuscript or R-table pointer (Section / page / footnote / table)

[7. REFERENCES]  (CORE in 4/6)
   - A bibliographic list for works newly cited in the response letter itself,
     formatted paper-style. The letter is treated as a free-standing scholarly
     document with its own References block, separate from the manuscript
     bibliography. [P1], [P2], [P3], [P4]

[8. RESPONSE-ONLY EXHIBITS]  (OPTIONAL — when new tables/figures are produced
   for the letter rather than the manuscript; 4/6)
   - "R"-prefixed sequence kept strictly separate from manuscript numbering:
     Tables R1, R2, …, R8 [P1]; Tables R.1–R.4 [P4]; Figure R.1 [P3];
     mirrored Tables R1–R7 that duplicate in-paper tables "for the ease of
     your reference" [P6].
   - DEFAULT naming: bare "R1" (majority across [P1], [P6]). The "R.1"
     period-style [P3], [P4] is a typesetting variant — pick one per letter.
   - Each R-exhibit carries a full caption, notes, significance stars, and
     Newey-West / double-clustered t-stats as appropriate.

[9. CLOSING]  (DEFAULT — include; 4/6)
   - Single ritualized sentence then sign-off (the majority pattern in
     [P2], [P3], [P4], [P6]).
   - With closing:
     * "Once again, we appreciate your detailed, constructive, and insightful
       feedback. The authors."  [P2], [P3]
     * "Once again, we are thankful for your insightful and constructive
       feedback, which have helped us improve the quality of our paper."  [P4]
     * "We would like to once again express our sincere appreciation for all
       your comments. They were very thoughtful and we really believe that
       your suggestions have helped improve the paper a lot."  [P6]
   - Without closing (occasional variant, [P1], [P5]): the letter simply ends
     on the last numbered response (no "Sincerely, Weikai Li" block).
   - DEFAULT: include a one-line closing ritual; it costs little and is the
     more common pattern.
```

## 3. Tone Register

**Deference level: HIGH (universal across all 6 letters).** The referee is consistently framed as the senior party and intellectual ally; the author never asserts epistemic peerage. Every comment — even minor typos — is opened with a gratitude formula.

**Warmth vs. formality:** Formal-professional with a thin layer of genuine warmth. Gratitude adjectives are deployed deliberately: "insightful," "constructive," "valuable," "thoughtful," "excellent suggestion," "really good comment." No first-name informality; always "the referee," "you," or "your." Authorial voice is consistently first-person plural ("we") — never singular.

**Hedging density: two distinct registers.**
- *Actions are stated declaratively without hedging* — "we have re-run," "we have added," "we report," "we have now moved," "we have now included." [P1], [P2]
- *Interpretations and mechanisms are heavily hedged* — "we conjecture," "might," "could," "may not be able to," "we think," "we believe," "could potentially," "it seems to suggest," "at a minimum," "we do not have causal evidence," "the hope is that." [P1], [P2], [P4]

**How tone shifts when conceding vs. pushing back:**

| Situation | Tone | Tell-tale signals |
|---|---|---|
| Full concede (referee is right, we act) | Warm, decisive, apologetic only if a true error | "Thank you for… We agree." "Following your suggestion, we have now…" "We apologize for the careless." |
| Concede-the-question, reframe via new evidence | Respectful, curious | "Thank you for raising these important issues." → introduces new tables → "Overall, the above comparison suggests…" |
| Pushback (mild to moderate) | Epistemically humble, evidentiary | "While we do not have the causal evidence here, the results at a minimum suggest…" "This seems to suggest…" "may not be necessarily inconsistent with…" |
| Defer to referee's preference | Explicit escape hatch | "We would be pleased to move… if you prefer." "if you prefer" |
| Concede a writing/error defect | Self-flagellating apology + immediate fix | "We apologize for the unclear writing…" "This was sloppy on our part; we apologize." |

**Universal rule (never violated in 6 letters):** The author **never says the referee is wrong**. Disagreement is performed via (a) reframing, (b) "while/although" concessions, (c) counter-evidence, or (d) deferential escape hatches — never via direct negation. [P1], [P2], [P3], [P4], [P5], [P6]

## 4. Comment-Handling Taxonomy

The author buckets referee points into the following observable categories. The **tell-tale phrasing** of each is the most reusable artifact in this guide. When drafting, classify each referee comment into one bucket and deploy that bucket's phrasing.

### (a) FULL CONCEDE + ACT (the dominant bucket)
The referee asks for X; the author does X (often more than X) and reports it. Common for: typo fixes, definition changes, tone-softening, missing references, moving text, adding controls.

- *Tell-tale:* "Following your suggestion, we have now [past participle]…" / "Per your suggestion, we have…" / "We exactly follow your suggestion in the revised version of the paper." / "We have followed the suggestions. Specifically, we added…" [P1], [P2], [P3], [P5], [P6]

### (b) CONCEDE-AND-AMPLIFY (exceed the ask)
Agree, then extend beyond what was asked — often adding a whole new section, multiple new tests, or running 1,000 repetitions when 20–30 were suggested.

- *Tell-tale:* "This was really an excellent suggestion. We have now added a new section…" [P6]; "We repeat this procedure 1,000 times…" [P5]; "we have conducted additional tests on the two results." [P4]
- Typical when the referee's suggestion opens a productive new angle (new "Empirical Framework" section, three new Tables 6–8, etc.). [P6]

### (c) CONCEDE THE QUESTION, REFRAME VIA NEW EVIDENCE
Agree the issue is important, then produce new tables / reinterpretations that preserve the paper's contribution. Used when the referee raises a puzzle that could threaten the main result.

- *Tell-tale:* "Thank you for raising these important issues. We agree that…" → new tables introduced → "Overall, the above comparison suggests that the difference between our paper and [Prior Work] is mainly caused by…" [P2], [P3]
- The reframing move: differences are framed as **measurement-driven, not sample-driven**; as **complementary, not competing**. [P3]

### (d) CONCEDE THE MECHANISM AND RUN WITH IT
When the referee proposes a specific channel, explicitly endorse it ("in line with your conjecture") and build new analysis around it.

- *Tell-tale:* "Thank you very much for providing the potential mechanism and empirical test… The underlying channel we have in mind for the return patterns is in line with your conjecture." [P1]; "Thank you for suggesting this test. We agree that the bond return predictability should be stronger when we use the change in CEI… Consistent with your conjecture, the alphas of the high-minus-low portfolios are more pronounced…" [P2]

### (e) CLARIFY-MISUNDERSTANDING (diplomatic)
Argue that two findings are not inconsistent, often with a formal decomposition or back-of-envelope calculation.

- *Tell-tale:* "The fact that X *unconditionally* may not be necessarily inconsistent with the interaction effect that we observe in Table 4." [P1]
- *Tell-tale:* "We agree with you and have changed our discussion of this test to reflect the ambiguity in what we should expect to see." [P6] — converts a disagreement on interpretation into a jointly-authored "ambiguity" framing.

### (f) PUSHBACK-WITH-EVIDENCE (mild to moderate)
Produce counter-evidence rather than assertion. The dominant move on threats to the main result. Hedged verbs carry the disagreement.

- *Tell-tale:* "If both are true, then it indeed poses an empirical threat… To evaluate this possibility, we obtain… This seems to suggest that the return predictability of ETF short ratio is not subsumed by the return predictability of ETF flows." [P4]
- *Tell-tale:* "However, the predictability of CEI for future bond returns does not come only from its predictability for future earnings news. As we show in Table 7…" [P2]

### (g) DEFER / PARTIAL CONCEDE / ACKNOWLEDGE LIMITATION
Don't fully adopt; go partway, give reasons, or defer the final choice to the referee.

- *Tell-tale:* "While we describe the results in the paper, we include this table to the Internet Appendix for two reasons. First, … Second, …" [P6] — adopt-the-spirit, decline-the-letter.
- *Tell-tale:* "We would be pleased to move the Fama-MacBeth table before the portfolio sorting table if you prefer." [P3]
- *Tell-tale:* "Since we do not have data on ETFs' creation/redemption basket lists, we cannot precisely determine… However, the hope is that we use the portfolio holdings of an ETF from the previous-quarter end." [P4]
- *Tell-tale:* "we do not have causal evidence suggesting that… For these reasons, we have removed the corresponding part… entirely." [P4] — concede-and-cut rather than argue.

### (h) PURE DISAGREE
**Not observed in any of the 6 letters.** If a future comment seems to require outright disagreement, escalate to the user (per the global `revision.md` DISAGREE rule) rather than fabricating a flat rejection.

## 6. Pushback Phrase Bank (Verbatim) — *Most Important Section*

The author's diplomatic pushback mechanism is **never a direct negation**. It takes the following forms, in order of ascending intensity.

### 6.1 Form 1 — "We agree that X, however Y" via new evidence (the primary pattern)

Concede the framing, then let a new table or calculation do the disagreeing. Pivot word: **"However"**.

- "We agree that for the bond market, the standard procedure of using realized returns might distort the true expected return… However, [new evidence follows]." [P3]
- "We agree that the Fama-MacBeth regression approach provides more robust evidence than the portfolio sorting analysis… However, empirical asset pricing papers typically present portfolio sorting results before the Fama-MacBeth regression results. This is perhaps influenced by classical papers such as Fama and French (1992)…" [P3] — appeal-to-tradition as a soft counter.
- "However, the predictability of CEI for future bond returns does not come only from its predictability for future earnings news. As we show in Table 7 of the revised manuscript…" [P2]
- "However, we do agree with you that any claim suggesting that these effects are related to RegFD is not appropriate. We no longer make any such claim." [P6] — concede the substantive objection while keeping the test.

### 6.2 Form 2 — "While / Although" concessions that preserve the contribution

- "Although we may not easily infer analysts' distorted incentives and/or behavioral biases from recommendation alone, we may be able to identify analysts' incentive distortions or behavioral bias more clearly by combining recommendations with anomaly signals." [P1] — concedes a limitation, then pivots to the paper's framing.
- "While we do not have the causal evidence here, the results at a minimum suggest that either consensus recommendations coordinate mutual fund managers' trading activities or mutual fund managers think in the same way as analysts do." [P1] — explicit epistemic humility that doubles as a defense of the contribution.
- "While we describe the results in the paper, we include this table to the Internet Appendix for two reasons. First, … Second, …" [P6] — soft "while" pivot, then enumerated justification.
- "We considered dropping these tests altogether, but believe that they are somewhat helpful in making one point…" [P6]
- "This is why we chose not to report this test in Table 2, but we mention this in footnote 14 in the paper, for the sake of completeness." [P6]

### 6.3 Form 3 — "Not necessarily inconsistent" / reframe rather than reject

- "The fact that recommendation alone has insignificant return predictability *unconditionally* may not be necessarily inconsistent with the interaction effect that we observe in Table 4." [P1] — diplomatic "you are not wrong, but…" without using "however" aggressively.

### 6.4 Form 4 — Bounded defense / quantitative reframing

Reframe an apparent contradiction as a quantitative bound.

- "We conjecture that the reason that the robust *t*-statistics do not reduce much might reflect the fact that we are dealing with a special case…" [P1] — offers a theoretical reason the referee's worry is less severe than feared, framed as conjecture.
- "This suggests that skilled analysts who do exploit anomalies when issuing recommendations are only a small fraction of all analysts, and their existence may not be able to fully eliminate mispricing." [P1] — reframes an apparent contradiction as a quantitative bound.

### 6.5 Form 5 — "Another important difference" / enlarge the framing

When asked to clarify contribution vs. a competing paper, enlarge the framing rather than dispute the premise.

- "In addition to our focus on expected bond returns, another important difference between our paper and their paper is that they use more coarse measures of firm exposure to climate regulatory risks." [P2]
- "Overall, the above comparison suggests that the difference between our paper and Bolton and Kacperczyk (2021) is mainly caused by whether one uses the level of carbon emissions or carbon intensity as the measure of carbon risk." [P2], [P3] — reframes "ad-hoc choices" (referee's framing) as "a substantive measurement choice" (author's framing).

### 6.6 Form 6 — Reconciliation rather than refutation (positioning vs. prior literature)

- "Thus our result is complimentary to that of Ilhan et al. (2021) as we show firms with higher carbon emissions intensity relative to other firms in the same industry have higher downside risk." [P3] (**sic — "complimentary"**)

### 6.7 Form 7 — Grant the threat before neutralizing it (validate → test → report)

The dominant move on Major Concerns. Stage the counter-test as a fair test of the referee's conjecture.

- "If both are true, then it indeed poses an empirical threat to the stand-alone return predictability of our ETF-based short ratio measure." [P4] — grants the threat.
- "To evaluate this possibility, we obtain month-end ETF shares outstanding from Bloomberg." [P4] — frames the counter-test as fair.
- "This non-result suggests that, at the monthly horizon, either (1) ETF flows are mostly driven by fundamental demand … or/and (2) operational shorting by APs accounts for a very small fraction of monthly ETF short ratios…" [P4]
- "This seems to suggest that the return predictability of ETF short ratio is not subsumed by the return predictability of ETF flows." [P4] — hedged verb carries the disagreement.
- "Given that ETF flows are virtually uncorrelated with ETF short ratios at the monthly interval, we do not expect ETF flows to weaken the predictive power of ETF short ratios…" [P4]
- "Second and more importantly, we find that controlling for ETF-based flow do not materially affect the return predictability of ETF-based SR." [P4]
- "The sharp contrast between ETF-based SR and ETF-based flow in predicting firm earnings news … is consistent with this interpretation." [P4] — uses the referee's own kernel-of-interest finding as differential evidence.

### 6.8 Form 8 — Agree-then-correct (factual correction with hedged framing)

For diplomatic corrections of fact / direction. Concede the parts that are right; introduce the correction with "we do note that…"; use italics on the corrective word.

- "We agree with the referee that the coefficients in the placebo test are of a large magnitude. We also agree that the p-value, although falls short of typical cutoffs, is close to being statistically significant at the 10% level. With all these said, we do note that the direction of the coefficient is *not* consistent with the results in the main analysis." [P5]
- Italics on "not," "more," "positive," "negative" to flag the technical correction without raising voice. [P5]
- Mechanism offered as interpretation, not assertion: "It is hard to pin down the exact mechanism…" [P5]
- External citation absorbing the interpretive load: "Some people call the COVID period recession 'shecession' (Alon et al., 2022)." [P5]

### 6.9 Form 9 — Deferential escape hatches ("if you prefer")

For decisions where the author would like to keep their choice but defers final say.

- "We would be pleased to move the Fama-MacBeth table before the portfolio sorting table if you prefer." [P3]
- "We would be pleased to move it to the main paper if you prefer." [P3]
- "We would be pleased to add this figure to the paper if you prefer." [P3]
- "Nevertheless, as you suggested, it is important to point out this data limitation to our readers." [P4] — defers while preserving the measure.

### 6.10 Form 10 — Concede-and-cut (when you can't defend, remove)

- "we do not have causal evidence suggesting that ETF ownership reduces mispricing for underlying stocks. For these reasons, we have removed the corresponding part (Section 5 and Table 11 of the original paper) entirely from the revised manuscript." [P4]

### Characteristic pushback vocabulary (recurring across letters)

> conjecture · might · may · "at a minimum" · "not necessarily inconsistent" · "in line with" · "consistent with" · "complimentary to" · "another important difference" · "this seems to suggest" · "we do not expect" · "virtually uncorrelated" · "do not materially affect" · "if you prefer" · "we would be pleased to" · "for the sake of completeness" · "the hope is that"

**The phrase "in line with"** is the author's preferred way to claim alignment with the referee without claiming the referee fully settled the question. [P1]

**Forbidden phrasings (never used in 6 letters):** "we respectfully disagree," "with all due respect," "you are wrong," "the referee is mistaken." Pushback is performed entirely through "However, …" + new result, reframing, or deference.

## 7. New-Evidence / Robustness Introduction Style

### 7.1 Purpose-first framing

New analysis is introduced with a **purpose clause** tied back to the referee's concern, then the method, then the pointer.

- "To test whether…, we…" [P1]
- "To investigate this possibility, we…" [P1]
- "To preserve as many stock-month observations as possible, we…" [P1]
- "To evaluate this possibility, we obtain month-end ETF shares outstanding from Bloomberg." [P4]
- "We first verify whether…" [P4]
- "We then include ETF monthly flows as…" [P4]
- "Following your suggestion, we conduct two additional robustness checks for our main results by using (1) model-implied bond returns and (2) returns to maturity as proxies for expected bond returns." [P3]
- "To estimate the model-implied bond return, we impose…" [P3]

### 7.2 Column-by-column walkthrough (the signature table-narration pattern)

When introducing a new table, walk through it column by column. Template: location pointer → one-sentence description → "In Column (1), we use… and find… In Column (2), we use… and also find…"

- "We report the replication results in Table A.14 of the Online Appendix. In Column (1), I use… and find… In Column (2), we use… and also find…" [P2]
- "In Column (1), we use the natural logarithm of carbon emissions level (ln(CO2)) and find a significant and positive coefficient, which is consistent with the positive carbon risk premium documented in Panel A of Table 8 of Bolton and Kacperczyk (2021)." [P2]
- Recurring across [P2], [P3], [P4].

### 7.3 Numerical results with t-stats inline

Every new result is paired with its t-statistic in parentheses.

- "the Fama-French three-factor alpha of the portfolio 'Short*Middle' is -0.42% and the alpha of the portfolio 'Short*Down' is -0.44%" [P1]
- "the difference… is 2.95% and is highly significant" [P1]
- "The result is reported in Table 6 of the revised paper. It clearly shows that analyst consensus recommendations are positively correlated with changes in mutual fund demand." [P1]
- "-0.60% per month (t-stat. = -4.20)" [P2]
- "The nine-factor alphas of the high-minus-low CEI portfolio are -0.20% (t-stat. = -3.18) and -0.40% (t-stat. = -4.20) per month for the model-implied bond returns and returns to maturity, respectively." [P3]
- "For example, the nine-factor alpha for the high-minus-low CEI portfolio is -0.60% per month (t-stat. = -4.20) for bonds with above-average stock-bond momentum spillover effect, and only -0.19% (t-stat. = -2.18) for bonds with below-average stock-bond momentum spillover effect." [P2]

### 7.4 Magnitude / economic significance always paired with a benchmark

- "a coefficient of 0.0243, compared to an unconditional likelihood of being the most accurate of 14.6%" [P6]
- "the coefficient on the connect dummy in this test is 0.01, relative to the unconditional probability of .0845" [P6]
- "In column (3), the coefficient of *Female\*Post* is 55.5% (i.e., 0.1591/0.2865) of the coefficient of *Forecast Age*." [P5]

### 7.5 R-table / R-figure naming convention

When new exhibits are produced for the letter itself (rather than the manuscript), they use a dedicated "R"-prefixed sequence kept strictly separate from manuscript numbering.

- **Tables R1, R2, … R8** — "R" for "response"; appended after the letter body, each with full caption and notes. [P1]
- Referenced as: "Table R1 to this response," "Table R2 to this response," … The "to this response" suffix distinguishes them from manuscript tables. [P1]
- **Tables R.1–R.4** — supporting tables at end of document. [P4]
- **Figure R.1** — response-only figure with caption. [P3]
- **Mirror tables R1–R7** — duplicate in-paper tables into the letter "for the ease of your reference" so the referee doesn't have to open the manuscript. [P6]
- **DEFAULT:** bare "R1" naming (majority across [P1], [P6]); the "R.1" period-style [P3], [P4] is a typesetting variant — pick one per letter.

### 7.6 Robustness demonstrated by enumerating alternatives

The pattern is: **primary table (in paper) + battery of R-tables (in letter)**.

- "We conduct several robustness checks… benchmark-adjusted mutual fund trading and the trading by all 13F institutions… Tables R5 and R6… Table R7." [P1]
- "Now, in the tables, we added a row at the bottom of the tables reporting the p-values of these significance tests. We also added short descriptions of these statistical tests." [P5]

### 7.7 Back-of-envelope calculations walked through step by step

Numbers tied to specific table cells. [P2]

- e.g., "3.07*0.0005", "15*0.069", "3.07*0.036" [P2]

### 7.8 Quoting revised manuscript prose as proof of completion

A typical response quotes the revised text verbatim, set off as an indented block quote, introduced by:

- "We discuss this issue on Page 14 and 15 of the revised manuscript, quoted as below:" [P4]
- "The relevant text is quoted as follows:" [P4]
- "Quoting from Section 4.1.2 in the paper:" [P6]
- "We replicate here Section 3.1 of the paper," "We paste below," "copied below for the ease of your reference" [P6]
- "Section 3.5 reads:" [P5]
- "we added the following discussion on page 13" [P5]
- "We have added the following discussion on page 3." [P5]

### 7.9 "Exceed the ask" pattern

When a referee suggests a modest robustness check, the author often exceeds the scope:

- "We repeat this procedure 1,000 times and plot the distribution of the estimated placebo coefficients of *Female\*Post* in Figure R1." [P5] — referee suggested 20–30 repetitions.
- Referee asked for F-test on one split → author added F-tests across all subgroup splits. [P5]
- "This was really an excellent suggestion. We have now added a new section to the paper, titled 'Empirical Framework' (Section 3 in the paper), to address it." [P6] — referee suggested one new test; author added three new Tables 6–8. [P6]

## 8. Formatting Conventions

### 8.1 Quoting the referee

- **Convention announced explicitly up front**, once, in the opening framing paragraph:
  - "Below, we reproduce your comments point by point in *italics* and then give our responses." [P1]
  - "We itemize your comments below in italics followed by our responses." [P2], [P3]
  - "Your comments are in italics followed by our responses." [P4]
  - "In each case, we start with the referee's comment, in italics." [P5]
  - "For ease of reference, we paste your comments below in italics, followed by our response to each comment." [P6]
- **Referee text reproduced VERBATIM** in *italics*, preserving original numbering, sub-bullets, comma style, and nested structure (1, 1a, 1b, 2.1, 2.2.1, etc.). Do NOT paraphrase.
- The italic block IS the comment — no "Referee:" prefix label needed (the italicization is the signal).
- The referee's own bold/italic section headings are preserved (e.g., ***General Assessment:***, ***Main Comments***). [P5]

### 8.2 Numbering scheme

- **Default: match the referee's original numbering exactly.** This is the spine of the response. [P1], [P2], [P3], [P5], [P6]
- Preserve nested structure: "1a, 1b" [P3]; "2.1, 2.2, 2.2.1, …, 2.3.4.5" [P6].
- For the **Summary of major changes** front-matter (separate from per-comment numbering), styles vary: Arabic "1., 2., 3." [P1]; roman "(I), (II), …" [P4]; "1) 2) 3)" [P6]. Pick one and stay consistent within the letter.
- Within a multi-part response, signpost with "First, … Second, … Finally, …" and land the takeaway with "Overall, …". [P2] The emphasis formula **"First, … Second and more importantly, …"** is characteristic. [P4]
- When a multi-part justification is needed, enumerate: "two reasons. First, … Second, …" [P6]; "First, … Second, … Finally, …" [P2].

### 8.3 Response labels

Three conventions, pick one per letter and stay consistent:

- **Bold "Response:" label** on its own line, then reply in roman text. [P1]
- **Plain prose reply** (no label) — the italic-block-then-response rhythm is enough. [P2], [P3], [P5], [P6] *(majority — recommended default)*
- **Bold topic label** of the form `Minor Concerns/Suggestions – [Topic]:` followed by italicized referee text, then indented reply. [P4]

### 8.4 Bold structural headings

Three tiers, used to organize the letter:

- Bold title block (manuscript title, journal number, "(Revised)", Date). [P1]
- Bold section headers: "Summary of major changes in this version of the paper:" [P1]; "Detailed Responses to the Reviewer's Comments" [P1]; "In response to comments in your report:" / "In response to minor comments in your report:" [P2]; "Point-to-point responses" [P4].
- Bold references/exhibit headers: "References," "Table R1: …" captions.
- **Inside responses**: bold labels generally NOT used for emphasis; responses run as plain prose. [P2], [P3], [P6] — EXCEPT [P4]'s topic-label convention.

### 8.5 Manuscript cross-references (precise pointers throughout)

Always give **specific locations**: Section + Page + Footnote + Table + Equation. Styles observed:

- "Table 4 of the revised paper," "Table 5 of the revised paper," "Table A1 (newly added) of the revised paper" [P1]
- "Section 4.4," "Section 4.7," "footnote 12," "footnote 15," "footnote 17," "footnote 25" [P1]
- "Section 3, Page 14 and 15 of the revised paper" [P1]
- **Dual-pointer (old paper → revised manuscript)** — a consistent habit of the [P2]/[P3] authorial voice: "Table 8 of the revised manuscript (Table A.10 of the old paper)," "Section 5.2.4," "page 23," "footnote 29" [P2]
- "(Section 5.2.4)", "(page 23)", "footnote 29 of the revised manuscript", "Table 4 Panel B" [P2]
- "pages 8 and 9 of the revised manuscript", "Panel D of Table A.6", "Section A.2 of the Online Appendix" [P3]
- "Footnote 26," "Page 3 and 4," "Section 3.1," "Eqn. (3) of the revised manuscript," "Table 7 of the revised paper" [P4]
- "column 3 of Table 2", "Panel A of Table 4", "page 3", "page 13", "Table A2 in the Internet Appendix", "Section 3.5" [P5]
- "Section 3.1," "Section 4.2," "Panel A of Table 11," "Table 1, Panel D," "footnote 13," "footnote 14," "Section 7.2" [P6]

**Distinguish "revised manuscript" / "current version of the paper" from "Online Appendix" / "Internet Appendix"** — tables moved to the OA/IA are explicitly flagged. [P2], [P3], [P6]

**Old-paper → new-paper migration log**: when many things moved, list them explicitly — "we have moved Section 3.1.1, Section 5.2.4, Section 5.2.5, Figure 2, Table 9, and Table 10 of the old paper to the Online Appendix." [P3]

### 8.6 Inline emphasis

- *t*-statistics in italics: "*t*-statistics" [P1]
- Italics for terms like "unconditionally," "only," "not," "more," "positive," "negative," and variable names: *Female\*Post*, *Forecast Age* [P1], [P5]
- Coefficients in roman with parenthetical italic *t*-stats.

### 8.7 Hypothesis / equation displays

- Revised hypotheses set out as indented quoted blocks: "H1: Carbon risk premium hypothesis: …" [P3]
- Equations numbered (1), (2) with LaTeX-style math. [P3]
- Short inline math: `$Coverage\_skill = Ln [(1+\# of skilled analysts)/(1+\# of total analysts)]$` [P1]

### 8.8 Footnotes inside the response letter

Used sparingly, for ancillary scholarly points, data provenance details, or side technical points. Mirror paper-style footnotes.

- Numbered footnotes using markdown link/anchor syntax `[[1]](#footnote-1)`, bodies collected before References. [P1]
- Two explanatory letter-footnotes (numbered 1 and 2). [P2]
- Footnote 1 on Bloomberg/CRSP shares-outstanding supplementation (data provenance). [P4]
- Footnote 1 on portfolio sorting / CEI; footnote 2 on Ilhan et al. industry-level measurement. [P3]

### 8.9 Embedded tables / figures

- Markdown tables for new regressions, placed inline immediately after the prose introducing them. [P5]
- Figure notes written in standard journal style ("this figure plots…"). [P5]
- Supporting tables appended at end of the same document: "Supporting tables are shown at the end of this document, if not already incorporated in the revised paper." [P4]

### 8.10 Bilateral credit attribution (optional but characteristic)

Crediting the referee **both in the response letter AND inside a manuscript footnote**:

- "We acknowledge your help in footnote 10." [P6]
- "In footnote 13, we acknowledge your help in pointing this out to us." [P6]

### 8.11 One-off / idiosyncratic formatting variants (do NOT propagate into defaults)

- **[P5] third-person address** ("We agree with the referee that…") — a one-off; 5/6 letters use second person ("you"/"your"). Default to second person. [P5]
- **[P5] placeholder tokens** ("page XX," "copy that paragraph," "copy that section") — these are draft-state artifacts in the source letter, NOT deliberate style. Always fill them in before sending; never reproduce them as a habit. [P5]
- **[P5] no summary block, no sign-off** — the austerity variant; do not imitate unless the revision is very light. [P5]
- **[P1] References block inside the letter** — adopted by [P1]–[P4]; recommended (treat the letter as a free-standing scholarly document). Not used in [P5], [P6].

## 9. Signature Micro-Habits & Recurring Templates

### 9.1 Opening rituals

- **"Thank you very much for…" / "We are grateful for…" / "We are sincerely grateful to…"** on essentially every comment. "Thank you very much" (not just "Thank you") is the default intensity in JFE letters. [P1], [P4]
- **Apology-for-delay framed as effort-signaler** (when revision took a long time): "We first apologize for the delay in getting back to you. In part, however, this reflects how seriously we have taken your report, and hence put in our best effort to do justice to this revision." [P6]
- **Front-loaded summary of major changes** before point-by-point work — a teaching/dispatching habit that lets the editor and referee skim. [P1], [P4], [P6] (3/6 — recommended default for heavy revisions)
- **Format-bridge announcement**: a single sentence stating the italics-and-response convention (see §8.1 for the five verbatim variants).

### 9.2 Concession rituals

- **"Per your suggestion, we have…"** / **"Following your suggestion, we have…"** as the standard bridge from request to action. [P1], [P2], [P3], [P5], [P6]
- **"We have now" + verb** as the signature action phrase — "we have now moved," "we have now included," "we have now added," "we have now renamed." Present-perfect with "now" signals the change is done. [P2], [P6]
- **Acknowledging-importance template**: "for pointing out this very important issue," "for the insightful observation and suggestion." [P1]
- **Explicit naming of new sections/tables added**: "a new table, Table 6, and a new section, Section 4.4, is added" — always tell the referee exactly where to find the change. [P1]

### 9.3 Alignment phrases

- **"in line with your conjecture," "consistent with your conjectured mechanism," "in line with our predictions," "Consistent with your conjecture"** — preferred ways to claim alignment with the referee without claiming the referee fully settled the question. [P1], [P2]

### 9.4 Hedging templates (the author's epistemic humility)

- **"at a minimum"** to lower-stakes a claim while preserving it: "the results at a minimum suggest that either consensus recommendations coordinate mutual fund managers' trading activities or mutual fund managers think in the same way as analysts do." [P1]
- **"This seems to suggest that…"** / **"This finding is particularly informative about…"** as preferred hedged-claim templates. [P4]
- **Explicit epistemic bounds** rather than flat claims: "may not be able to fully eliminate," "does not fully eliminate," "partially explain," "we do not have causal evidence," "the hope is that." [P1], [P4]

### 9.5 Emphasis formulas

- **"First, … Second and more importantly, …"** — characteristic emphasis formula. [P4]
- **"Based on your comments and to be consistent with Table 7, …"** — explicitly invoking consistency with the referee's logic as the justification for a global change. [P4]

### 9.6 Takeaway / paragraph-closer

- **"Overall,"** as the standard paragraph-closer to land the takeaway after a multi-step evidence walk. [P2]
- **"Overall, the above comparison suggests that…"** as the standard reconciliation closer. [P2], [P3]

### 9.7 Helpfulness framing

- **"for the ease of your reference"** / **"for your convenience"** / **"for your reference"** / **"for the sake of completeness"** — recurring closing rationale whenever a manuscript chunk, table, or figure is duplicated into the letter. [P6]
- **"reproduced below for your convenience"** / **"described in response to your suggestion 2.2.1 below"** [P6]

### 9.8 Restating the referee's comment as a compliment

Before answering a substantial suggestion:

- "This was really an excellent suggestion." [P6]
- "This is another really good comment." [P6]
- "Thank you for pointing this out." [P1], [P6]

### 9.9 Quantification habit

- Every new result is paired with its t-statistic in parentheses. [P1], [P2], [P3], [P4]
- Concessions quantified: when the referee said "20/30" repetitions, the author responds with "1,000 times"; when the referee asked for "economic magnitudes," the author supplies a precise ratio (55.5%, 49 days, 86 days). [P5]

### 9.10 Closing rituals

- **"Once again, we appreciate your detailed, constructive, and insightful feedback."** then **"The authors."** [P2], [P3]
- **"Once again, we are thankful for your insightful and constructive feedback, which have helped us improve the quality of our paper."** [P4]
- **"We would like to once again express our sincere appreciation for all your comments. They were very thoughtful and we really believe that your suggestions have helped improve the paper a lot."** [P6]
- **No formal sign-off** — letter ends on the last numbered response. [P1], [P5] (occasional variant; minority 2/6)

### 9.11 References in the letter itself

- Listed separately from the manuscript bibliography — the author treats the letter as a free-standing scholarly document. [P1], [P2], [P3], [P4]

### 9.12 Characteristic vocabulary (recurring across letters)

> "greatly improved" · "considerably improve" · "carefully addressed" · "extensive analyses" · "constructive and insightful" · "insightful observation" · "excellent suggestion" · "really good comment" · "important difference" · "incremental contribution" · "back-of-envelope calculation" · "deploy" (tables/discussions deployed to the appendix) [P3] · "the old paper" / "the revised manuscript" (binary tense) · "Online Appendix" / "Internet Appendix" (capitalized) · "robust" · "virtually unchanged" · "materially affect" · "consistent with" · "for completeness" · "Following your suggestion" · "We agree" · "We apologize"

## 10. Do / Don't Quick-Reference

### DO

- **Open every reply with a "Thank you for…" formula** before any substance. [P1]–[P6]
- **Use first-person plural ("we")** throughout — never first-person singular.
- **Address the referee in the second person** ("you," "your"). [P1], [P2], [P3], [P4], [P6]
- **State actions declaratively in present-perfect**: "we have now moved / included / revised / corrected / renamed." [P2], [P6]
- **Front-load a Summary of Major Changes** before the point-by-point section in heavy-revision letters. [P1], [P4], [P6]
- **Reproduce referee comments VERBATIM in italics**, preserving original numbering and nested structure. [P1]–[P6]
- **Follow the referee's own ordering** — never reorder or regroup.
- **Pin every change to a precise manuscript location**: Section + Page + Footnote + Table + Equation. [P1]–[P6]
- **Quote the revised manuscript prose** verbatim (indented block quote) as proof of completion. [P4], [P6]
- **Pair every numerical result with its t-statistic** in parentheses. [P1]–[P4]
- **Pair every magnitude with a benchmark** (unconditional probability, baseline coefficient). [P5], [P6]
- **Walk through new tables column by column**: "In Column (1), we use… and find…" [P2], [P3], [P4]
- **Use "R"-prefixed tables/figures** (Table R1, Figure R.1) for response-only exhibits; keep them separate from manuscript numbering. [P1], [P3], [P4], [P6]
- **Pivot pushback on "However" / "While" / "Although"** + new evidence — let tables do the disagreeing. [P1]–[P5]
- **Use "at a minimum," "seems to suggest," "may not be necessarily inconsistent"** to hedge claims while preserving them. [P1], [P4]
- **Exceed the ask when a suggestion opens a productive angle** (more repetitions, more subgroup splits, new section). [P5], [P6]
- **Apologize explicitly for prior-version defects**: "We apologize for the unclear writing…" / "This was sloppy on our part; we apologize." then state the fix. [P3], [P6]
- **Defer final choice to the referee** with "We would be pleased to… if you prefer." [P3]
- **Reframe differences as measurement-driven or complementary**, not as ad-hoc choices or competition. [P2], [P3]
- **List newly cited works in a References block** at the end of the letter (treat the letter as free-standing scholarly document). [P1]–[P4]
- **Use "Overall," to land the takeaway** after a multi-step evidence walk. [P2]
- **Distinguish "revised manuscript" from "Online Appendix"** explicitly when things moved. [P2], [P3], [P6]
- **Include a one-line closing ritual** (e.g., "Once again, we appreciate…"). [P2], [P3], [P4], [P6] (majority default)
- **Fill in every placeholder** ("page XX," "copy that paragraph") before sending. [P5 anti-pattern]

### DON'T

- **Never say "you are wrong," "the referee is mistaken," "we respectfully disagree," or "with all due respect."** Pushback is performed entirely through "However, …" + new result, reframing, or deference. [P1]–[P6]
- **Never use direct negation to reject a referee point.** Use bounded defense, reframing, or escape hatches instead. [P1]–[P6]
- **Never paraphrase the referee's comment** — reproduce it verbatim. [P1]–[P6]
- **Never reorder or regroup referee points** — follow the referee's own ordering and numbering. [P1]–[P6]
- **Never assert interpretations without hedging.** Use "we conjecture," "might," "may," "seems to suggest," "at a minimum," "the hope is that." [P1], [P2], [P4]
- **Never claim the referee fully settled a question.** Use "in line with your conjecture" / "consistent with" instead. [P1]
- **Never introduce a new table without a column-by-column walkthrough and a precise pointer.** [P2]
- **Never report a coefficient without its t-statistic or economic-magnitude benchmark.** [P1]–[P6]
- **Never use contractions or first-name informality with the referee.** Always "the referee," "you," "your." [P1]–[P6]
- **Never invent a flat "pure disagree."** If a comment seems to require outright disagreement, escalate to the user (per global `revision.md` DISAGREE rule) rather than fabricating a rejection.
- **Never leave an action ambiguous.** If you did X, say "we have now [past participle] X" and point to the location. If you didn't, explain via the "two reasons" template or defer with "if you prefer."
- **Never mix numbering styles within a letter.** Pick Arabic / roman / referee-native once and stay consistent.
- **Never mix response-label conventions within a letter** — choose bold-"Response:" OR plain prose OR topic-label, and stay consistent.
- **Never forget the dual-pointer habit** when many things moved: give both old-paper and revised-manuscript locations. [P2], [P3]
- **Never auto-correct a source verbatim quote** (e.g., reproduce [P3]'s "complimentary" sic when quoting it as an exemplar).
- **Never leave placeholder tokens** ("page XX," "copy that section") in a finished letter. [P5]

**Setup.** Manuscript: *"Analyst Sentiment and the Cross-Section of Stock Returns"* (hypothetical). Referee Comment 3 reads:

> **3.** *The main result that analyst sentiment predicts future returns is interesting. However, I am concerned that the predictability is driven by the well-known short-term momentum effect. Could the authors control for past 6-12 month returns (Jegadeesh and Titman, 1993) and report whether the sentiment effect survives?*

**Model response (in the author's voice):**

> **Response:** Thank you very much for pointing out this very important issue. We agree that it is important to understand whether the return predictability of analyst sentiment is distinct from the well-known momentum effect. [P1] To evaluate this possibility, [P4] we include past returns (over the 6-, 9-, and 12-month horizons à la Jegadeesh and Titman, 1993) as an additional control in our baseline Fama-MacBeth regression. The results are reported in Table R1 to this response. [P1] In Column (1), we use the 6-month past return and find that the coefficient on analyst sentiment remains 0.045 (t-stat. = 3.12), virtually unchanged from the 0.047 (t-stat. = 3.28) reported in Column (1) of Table 4 of the revised paper. [P2] In Column (2), we use the 12-month past return and also find that the sentiment coefficient stays significant at 0.042 (t-stat. = 2.95). [P2] Second and more importantly, [P4] we find that controlling for past returns does not materially affect the return predictability of analyst sentiment. [P4] Given that past returns and analyst sentiment are virtually uncorrelated in our sample (correlation = 0.04), we do not expect momentum to weaken the predictive power of sentiment. [P4] Overall, the above comparison suggests that the return predictability of analyst sentiment is not subsumed by the momentum effect. [P2] Following your suggestion, [P1] we have now added this control to Table 4 of the revised manuscript (Table 3 of the old paper) [P2] and discuss the results on page 17, quoted as below: [P4]
>
> > "[…] After controlling for past 6-, 9-, and 12-month returns following Jegadeesh and Titman (1993), the coefficient on *Sentiment* remains statistically significant at the 1% level (t-stat. = 3.12), suggesting that analyst sentiment carries return-predictive information incremental to the momentum effect."
